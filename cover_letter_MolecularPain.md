Dear Editor,

Please find enclosed our manuscript entitled **“Computational re-analysis of convergent Wnt/β-catenin and pyroptosis signaling in neuropathic pain”** for consideration as an Original Research Article in *Molecular Pain*.

*Molecular Pain* seeks to advance the molecular, cellular, and mechanistic understanding of pain. Our study addresses this scope directly: it interrogates, at single-cell resolution, whether Wnt/β-catenin and NLRP3-inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis converge in specific spinal-cord cell types in neuropathic pain, and it nominates tractable pharmacological candidates (MCC950, disulfiram, XAV939) targeting these nodes. The work is therefore a strong fit for the journal’s readership, which is specifically interested in pain mechanisms at the molecular level.

Using only publicly available data (one rat bulk RNA-seq cohort, GSE217932; one mouse spared-nerve-injury single-cell RNA-seq cohort, GSE134003) together with STRING, DrugBank, and PDB resources, we show that pyroptosis signaling localizes to microglia and macrophages rather than astrocytes, that astrocyte-specific Wnt–pyroptosis co-activation is not supported by current data, and that bulk Wnt/β-catenin enrichment is absent after correction (NES ≈ −1.6, nominal p ≈ 0.03). A descriptive three-tier classification highlights nine Tier-1 drug candidates. In keeping with the journal’s emphasis on rigor and transparency, we explicitly report negative and null findings and provide a per-module reproducibility/limitation self-audit (Supplementary Figure S2).

The scientific design, all computational analyses, and every reported result were conceived and executed solely by the author; the tool performed no analysis and contributed no data, code, or interpretation. No third party submitted this manuscript on the author’s behalf.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by any other journal. The single author has approved the submission. Because the study is a purely computational re-analysis of publicly available, de-identified datasets, no new human-participant, human-data, or animal-work ethics approval was required (stated under *Ethical considerations*).
